4. Narrative Quality Assessment

Case PV-101 – Nausea

Available information: A 34-year-old female from India reportedly experienced nausea three hours after use of Velunex and recovered. The case is marked not serious.

Quality assessment: The event and reported outcome are clear. Dose, route, indication, treatment dates, event duration, management, and relevant medical history are missing.

Recommended follow-up: Clarify product exposure details, event duration, treatment, medical history, and reporter information where available.

Case PV-102 – Loss of Consciousness

Available information: A 61-year-old male from India reportedly experienced loss of consciousness two days after use of Velunex. The patient is recovering, and emergency hospital admission is reported.

Quality assessment: The event and hospital admission are documented, but clinical details are limited. The seriousness criteria and clinical circumstances require further review.

Recommended follow-up: Obtain event circumstances, clinical findings, hospital admission and discharge details, treatment, concomitant medicines, medical history, and exact product exposure dates.

Case PV-103 – Mild Rash

Available information: A 27-year-old female from the United Kingdom reportedly developed a mild rash four days after use of Velunex and recovered. No rechallenge was reported.

Quality assessment: The reported event and outcome are available. The rash description is insufficient for more specific coding. No rechallenge does not establish or exclude causality.

Recommended follow-up: Clarify rash location, appearance, duration, treatment, allergy history, concomitant medicines, and product exposure details.

Case PV-104 – Appendicitis

Available information: A patient from India reportedly developed appendicitis three weeks after use of Velunex and recovered. Hospitalisation is reported. Patient age is missing, sex is unknown, and medical history is incomplete.

Quality assessment: The event term is explicit, but important patient and clinical details are missing. The temporal association alone does not establish that Velunex caused appendicitis.

Recommended follow-up: Obtain patient details where available, diagnostic findings, hospitalisation information, medical history, concomitant medicines, and product exposure dates.

6. General Narrative Quality Recommendations

A high-quality adverse event narrative should clearly describe:

1. Relevant patient characteristics.
2. Suspect product details, including dose, route, indication, and treatment dates where available.
3. Event onset and clinical progression.
4. Relevant medical history and concomitant medicines.
5. Treatment provided and clinical findings.
6. Seriousness criteria and outcome.
7. Relevant investigations and follow-up information.
8. Clear separation of reported facts from assumptions.

Missing information should be documented as unknown or not provided rather than inferred.
